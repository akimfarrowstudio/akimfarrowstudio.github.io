01_Foundations

My teaching philosophy is rooted in the belief that technical precision is the catalyst for creative liberation. In the studio, I foster an environment where rigorous industrial training meets experimental inquiry. I believe that students must first understand the molecular behavior of their materials—the heat of the crucible, the tension of the weld—before they can meaningfully subvert those processes. My role is to provide the structural scaffolding that allows for radical student autonomy and bold, risk-taking approaches to sculpture.

02_Technical Mastery

Foundry work, mold making, and metal fabrication are demanding disciplines that require absolute presence. I emphasize building strong foundational skills through repetitive practice and deep material engagement. In my classroom, safety protocols and equipment mastery are not mere prerequisites; they are the essential grammar of the medium. By instilling a culture of technical excellence, I empower students to move beyond the fear of the industrial space, transforming heavy machinery into an extension of their creative intent.

03_Studio Culture

I am committed to creating inclusive, high-stakes learning environments that welcome diverse voices and backgrounds. I operate under a mentorship model where I act as a technical guide rather than a final authority. The studio is a site of collective problem-solving; I encourage peer-to-peer learning and collaborative fabrication, mirroring the real-world dynamics of large-scale sculpture production. Every student brings a unique cultural and conceptual lens, and the studio must remain a space where those lenses are sharpened through shared effort.

04_Critical Thinking

Technical skill without conceptual weight is merely decoration. I challenge my students to interrogate their material choices: Why bronze? Why steel? What is the historical and cultural gravity of the industrial process? By connecting physical labor to broader artistic conversations—Afrofuturism, industrial labor history, and contemporary social structures—I guide students to produce work that is technically sophisticated and intellectually resonant.

05_Professional Practice

The transition from student to practitioner requires more than just artistic vision. I prepare students for the practical realities of a career in the arts, emphasizing high-fidelity documentation, portfolio development, and studio management. From budgeting for material costs to navigating fabrication contracts, I ensure that my students graduate with the technical confidence and professional literacy required to sustain a long-term practice in the contemporary landscape.
